# Now make your own record

Choose one of two equally good ways:

- **Your own safe observation.** Think of something you have already noticed in the sky. You do not need to go outside now, and you must not look directly at the Sun.
- **A published NASA photograph and its caption.** If you have no observation of your own, use the verified source below. Record *information from the photograph and its caption*; do not claim you saw it yourself.

**Source for your activity (the complete account is here; you do not need to open the link):** NASA published Bill Ingalls's photograph “Supermoon in Washington.” NASA's caption says that **on June 23, 2013, in Washington, USA, the Moon was rising behind the Washington Monument**. This is **a real photograph with a caption**, not a made-up learning drawing. The caption does not give the exact time or the weather. [Check the original NASA source](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/supermoon-washington/).

**Your task:** In *your own words*, make a record of what you learn from this source. Say what is shown, the date, the general place and source. Explain that you read or heard the photograph's caption, and honestly mark unknown conditions. Do not claim that you took the photo or observed the event yourself. If you chose your own memory of the sky, use the same template and clearly say how you observed it.

Answer the five helpful questions. If you cannot read yet, ask someone nearby **to read aloud the NASA caption and the questions exactly as given here**; do not change or invent information.
