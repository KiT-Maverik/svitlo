# Skill Taxonomy Review

**Original review date:** 2026-10-29  
**Artifact:** [Geography — Skills Taxonomy](../skills/taxonomy.md)  
**Editorial reference:** [Geography taxonomy PR #66](https://github.com/KiT-Maverik/svitlo-web/pull/66)

This is the single dated historical record of **editor-agreed decisions** for the Geography taxonomy. It was created at the editor's explicit request during the initial editorial cycle, not because a new textbook generation or implemented taxonomy was assumed. The linked `taxonomy.md` remains the sole canonical source for current names, descriptions, hierarchy and Code references. Outcomes below concern editorial choices, **not approval of implementation or assessment evidence**.

## 2026-10-29 — Initial taxonomy editorial decisions

### Subject scope and learning architecture — APPROVED

**Alternatives considered:** mirror an external national standards framework in the taxonomy, or keep a program-independent geography competency catalog. **Decision:** use the latter. Geography incorporates cartography, geology, meteorology, water systems, biogeography, human geography, and geographic environment–society relations. The taxonomy lists reusable competencies; **Lessons** teach one or several Skills, while **Programs** select and sequence Lessons. A future Program may follow the National Geography Standards without requiring corresponding Domains or forcing their learning depth into Skill definitions. Standards are useful for detecting genuinely missing competencies, not automatically generating one Skill per benchmark. Skills have no fixed age/grade ceiling.

### Distinct geology and surface-process fields — APPROVED

**Alternatives considered:** retain a single `Earth Surface and Land Processes` Domain spanning the planet's interior, minerals, rocks, relief and weathering; or distinguish the fields. **Decision:** split it into **`Geology`** (materials, tectonics, geological history and deposits) and **`Landforms and Surface Processes`** (relief, soil and surface transformation). The prior exceptionally large mixed Domain reflected two legitimately distinct fields. Domain sizes need not be equal; this creates **no separate Geology textbook**.

### Fundamental geographic objects — APPROVED

**Alternatives considered:** rely on scattered names and examples inside process-focused Groups, or explicitly teach recognition and classification of geographic features. **Decision:** add Domain **`Geographic Objects`**. Use concise child Group names **`Classification`**, **`Global Geographic Divisions`**, **`Land and Water`**, and **`Human-Made Objects`**, without repeating the parent's `Geographic Objects` qualifier. Teach how features and regions are recognized, classified and located; use named rivers, islands or settlements as content and assessment examples rather than generating one Skill for every named place.

The editor specifically required distinguishing **continents / continental landmasses** from historically defined **parts of the world** and recognizing the variation among geographic classification conventions (for example, Eurasia compared with Europe and Asia). Locating continents, parts of the world and oceans on a globe is a meaningful transferable competency. Which countries or regional examples are introduced, and when, belongs to a Program.

### Time zones and spatial reference — APPROVED

**Alternatives considered:** treat local time as an unstructured detail of spatial orientation, or make time systems a coherent Group. **Decision:** retain **`Time Zones`** as a Group, with distinct understanding of local **civil** versus local **solar** time, longitude-related solar timing, legally defined UTC offsets and calendar boundaries. Relative and absolute location are independently meaningful abilities; a comparison task does not alone justify an additional composite Skill.

### Skills versus individual practice tasks — APPROVED

**Alternatives considered:** create separate Skills for each way of drawing a plan, representing a legend, using a familiar route, testing a mineral or recognizing a named geographic feature; or use stable transferable competencies. **Decision:** prefer one **`Create maps`** Skill and one **`Use mental maps for spatial orientation`** Skill, with exercises as variants rather than separate identities. Consolidate over-fragmented mineral-property and coastal-form recognition into broader usable competencies. Keep independently meaningful abilities separate, including ocean tides versus currents and weathering versus erosion versus deposition. Use the canonical **A-without-B** test rather than mechanically splitting or joining titles that contain conjunctions.

### Geographic information, cultural and economic coverage — APPROVED

**Alternatives considered:** treat all geographic tools as isolated tasks, or classify coherent reusable competence families. **Decision:** represent **`Geospatial Technologies`** as a Group encompassing spatial imagery, positioning and layer-based analysis, without duplicating the implementation of GIS software from Informatics. Maintain geographic interpretations of **`Cultural Geography`** and **`Economic Geography`** as Groups with independently meaningful underlying Skills rather than one vaguely assessable Skill named after an entire discipline. Cover distribution and diffusion of culture, economic specialization, regional interdependence and human–environment relations where they express genuine geographic abilities.

Rename unnecessarily verbose Groups when context is already supplied by the parent; eliminate overlapping Skill descriptions or misplaced membership instead of inflating the taxonomy.

### Foundational weather, climate, water and resources — APPROVED

**Alternatives considered:** teach only the contrast between weather and climate, or explicitly define both. **Decision:** retain **`Explain the concept of weather`** and **`Explain the concept of climate`** as distinct Skills, without requiring a redundant third comparison Skill merely because a Question can contrast them. Expand coverage of atmospheric processes, climate patterns, groundwater and frozen-water geography while keeping related processes distinguishable when mastery of one does not imply mastery of another.

For natural resources, understanding what makes something a resource is distinct from understanding replenishment and depletion. Renewable does **not** mean inexhaustible.

### Aquatic ecosystems and natural-hazard lessons — APPROVED

**Alternatives considered:** leave freshwater and marine environments implicit within terrestrial-biome coverage, or represent their geographic distinction directly. **Decision:** include one independent **`Distinguish marine and freshwater ecosystems`** Skill in **`Natural Regions and Biogeography → Ecosystems`**, allowing brackish transitional environments instead of a rigid binary.

The editor also decided that a single **Lesson** may introduce floods, landslides and other natural hazards. Their joint appearance in one Lesson does **not** warrant one Skill per named hazard. Relevant existing geographic hazard and process Skills can be taught together; emergency-response procedures are primarily Safety content.

### Exception-review discipline and ownership — APPROVED

**Alternatives considered:** equalize counts or copy broad standard headings mechanically, versus evaluate subject meaning and competency identity. **Decision:** use [Skills Taxonomy Exception Review](../../../../studio/skills/review-skills-taxonomy-exceptions.skill.md) to flag asymmetry, duplicate/repeated names, unnecessarily long titles, near-duplicate competencies, gaps and incorrect Domain/Group placement. Counts are **diagnostic evidence, not quotas**. An editorial exception report is not an approved decision. Only editor-agreed revisions change the canonical taxonomy; no per-item review-history files or per-entity Definition documents are created.
