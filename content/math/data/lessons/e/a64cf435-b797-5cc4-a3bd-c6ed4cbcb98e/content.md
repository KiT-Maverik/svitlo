# What Do We Know? What Must We Find? — Lesson Content

## Section 1 — Listen for the question

1. **text**: “A box holds six pencils. Three more pencils are put inside. How many pencils are there now?”
2. **scene (proposed)**: [Story Fact Finder](./scenes/storyFactFinder.scene-spec.md). Learner categorizes “six first”, “three added”, and “how many now?” as given information or unknown. Confirmation: “Six and three are known; the new total is what we need.”
3. **markdown**: “First ask: what do we know, and what do we need to find? We do not have to calculate yet.”

## Section 2 — The relationship matters

1. **text**: “Mira has eight stickers. She gives two away. How many remain?” Offer “two came in” versus “two went away”.
2. **scene (proposed)**: New-story Fact Finder highlights the action and asks which quantity is unknown.
3. **text**: Feedback for “came in”: “Giving away removes stickers from the collection. Read the action, not just the number.”

## Section 3 — Not every detail is a quantity

1. **markdown**: “A green basket contains four pears. Two more pears are added. How many pears are there now?” Green describes the basket but does not change the counts.
2. **scene (proposed)**: Sort “green basket”, “four pears”, “two more pears” and “how many now” into descriptive detail, useful known quantities and unknown. Wrong feedback: “Color helps us picture the story, but cannot tell us the number of pears.”
3. **text**: “Numbers matter together with what they describe.”

## Section 4 — Transfer to a new story

1. **text**: “Seven cups stand on a shelf. A helper puts five more cups there. Say which numbers are known, describe the change, and name what is still unknown.”
2. **text**: Reveal: “Known: seven, five, and the adding action. Unknown: number of cups afterward.” Do not demand a calculation or claim independent problem-solving mastery.
3. **markdown**: “Remember: **given facts → relationship → question**.”

## Access and production constraints

Every Section follows top-to-bottom Resource order. Fact Finder is a proposed scene, not a Question bank. Provide short accessible text alternatives to all visuals; offer keyboard and tap selection instead of mandatory drag. Translations must preserve the different meanings of “more” and “give away”, have equivalent feedback in all supported locales and honor RTL relationships, zoom, contrast, reduced-motion and offline completeness. No scoring, persistent results, Note events or invented APIs.
