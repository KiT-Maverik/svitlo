      # My First Sky Journal
      
      Imagine a friend who did not see the sky you looked at. How could you help them understand **what you noticed**?
      
      Today you will make a short record about the sky. You can use a few words, symbols, a drawing, or an explanation spoken aloud. You do not need long sentences.

      ~~~~
      ## Observed detail, impression, or guess?
      
      Imagine a **made-up drawing for this lesson** showing the Moon and two clouds. It is not a photograph of the real sky.
      
      - **Something you can check:** “The drawing shows the Moon and two clouds.”
      - **An impression:** “The sky is beautiful.” That is how you may feel, but it does not identify a detail you can observe.
      - **A guess:** “The Moon made the clouds appear.” The drawing provides no evidence for that cause.
      
      If you only read a description, say **“the description says”**, not “I saw it in the sky.”
      
      ### Try it
      
      Which sentence gives your friend something they can check: “There are two clouds in the drawing” or “The Moon called the clouds”? Say or point to your answer.
      
      **Check your answer:** The first sentence describes what is shown. The second makes up a cause. If you chose the wrong one, try again using only a detail you can check.

      # Five helpful questions
      
      To make your record clear to someone else, give short answers:
      
      1. **What?** Which object or detail did you notice?
      2. **When?** Morning, daytime, evening, or a known date.
      3. **Where or from what source?** A general place (“from the window”) **or a source** (“from a learning description”). You do not need to give your exact address or GPS location.
      4. **How?** Did you see it yourself, read about it, look at a captioned picture, or listen to a description?
      5. **Conditions?** Was it clear or cloudy, or was something in the way? Include only details you know.
      
      If you do not know something, say **“not stated”** rather than making it up. Symbols, drawings, or a short spoken explanation can also make a good record.
      
      ~~~~

      ~~~~
      ## How can you correct an unclear record?
      
      **Incomplete record:** “The sky was beautiful yesterday.”
      
      **Why is that not enough?** “Beautiful” is an impression. The detail someone noticed, where and how they learned about it, and the conditions are **not stated**. Do not add clouds, a window, or weather conditions if you do not remember them or cannot confirm them from the source.
      
      **How can you make it clearer?** Recall a real detail or return to a reliable source. Record only what you can support; mark unknown details as “not stated.” Without a specific **what** and an honest source or method, this is not yet an observation record.
      
      **A separate made-up example:** The learning description “Evening Sky” above **says** the Moon and two clouds were visible in the evening. These details come from that description. They do not correct the statement about yesterday and are not your personal observation.
      
      **Your turn:** Name one missing field in the first sentence. Then say or point to one detail from the made-up description and name its source. If something cannot be confirmed, say “not stated” instead of making it up. Use words, drawings, or symbols — handwriting and sentence length are not graded.

      # What is different between the records?
      
      Here are **two made-up records for practice**, not real observations.
      
      **A:** “During the day, someone noticed clouds from a yard. They looked without instruments. No other conditions were stated.”
      
      **B:** “In the evening, someone noticed the Moon and clouds from a yard. They looked without instruments. It was cloudy.”
      
      **Difference 1:** A says “during the day”; B says “in the evening.”  
      **Difference 2:** The Moon is **mentioned** only in B.
      
      **Be careful:** If A does not mention the Moon, that does not prove the Moon was absent. The records also do not show *why* the details are different.
      
      **Your turn:** Name one difference and point to or say which words support it.
