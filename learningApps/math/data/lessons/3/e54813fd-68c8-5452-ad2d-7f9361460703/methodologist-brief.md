## Educational purpose

Read the ten digits 0–9 as symbols that represent numbers and corresponding quantities. Program-neutral and independently teachable.

## Target audience

**Grade 1 (primary audience)**, early readers using short prompts, countable illustrations, and optional narration. No requirement for mouse use, hearing, or fluent reading.

## Prerequisites

Count 0–9 objects and connect spoken number words with quantities. Provide an optional introductory reminder, not an assumption of demonstrated mastery.

## Expected learning outcomes

1. The digit 3 represents three apples, though it is one written symbol.
2. 0 means no balls; 1 one ball; 6 six balls.
3. Each digit 0–9 can label its matching set of dots.
4. A single 7 labels a complete seven-block collection.

- Explain a new instance using the underlying number relationship, not merely identify a familiar arrangement.

## Essential subject matter and instructional depth

Only single digits and their object models, no positional notation or multidigit arithmetic. Verified model examples:

- Three apples → 3.
- Empty basket → 0 balls.
- 6 dots → 6; 9 dots → 9.
- Seven blocks match digit 7.

## Conceptual progression and dependencies

1. One mark for three: Count three apples and select a digit card among 2, 3 and 5.
2. The empty basket: Switch basket contents between zero, one and six balls; choose matching digit.
3. Digit gallery: Explore accessible cards 0–9 with spoken and printed number words, ten-frame models.
4. The shelf label: Add or remove blocks until a new shelf contains exactly seven blocks.

## Misconceptions, examples and boundaries

Treating the shape of a digit as an amount, confusing 0 with an object, mistaking 6 for 9. Feedback must point to the relevant concrete counting evidence. No score, timed drill, or inference of mastered competence.
