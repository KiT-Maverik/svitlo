## Educational purpose

Help children reconstruct an unknown _starting whole_ in a subtraction. When the amount removed and the amount remaining are known, putting those two parts back together finds the original whole. This is the inverse relation behind □ − 4 = 7, so □ = 7 + 4 = 11. A reliable answer is checked by substitution into the **original subtraction**, not by accepting a plausible sum.

## Target audience

Children aged approximately 6–8 (grades 1–2) who understand concrete subtraction, addition within 20 and the equals sign as a relationship. Provide support for emerging readers, numeracy differences and nonvisual participation; use counters and short bilingual-ready sentences before formal component vocabulary.

## Prerequisites

- Count, add and subtract whole-number amounts within 20, using counters if needed.
- Interpret “taken away,” “left,” and “started with” as distinct quantities.
- Know that whole = removed part + remaining part; understand a blank denotes one unknown number.
- Recognize equality rather than reading the equals sign as a command.

## Expected learning outcomes

- Identify the missing first number in □ − 4 = 7 as the _starting whole_, not what was removed.
- Reconstruct the starting whole by combining 4 removed and 7 remaining: 7 + 4 = 11.
- Model the part–whole reconstruction physically, visually or with an equivalent accessible number record.
- Verify the candidate in the original equality 11 − 4 = 7 and distinguish this calculation from finding an unknown subtrahend.

## Essential subject matter

The minuend is the quantity from which another amount is subtracted. For nonnegative whole numbers, if □ − b = c, the original amount is b + c. In the story “Four were taken away and seven remain,” the removed group and remaining group together represent the original eleven; they are **not two newly added collections** unrelated to the story. The written check is 11 − 4 = 7. An unknown subtrahend instead has a known starting whole and remainder; its inverse rule is different. For this age group use totals up to 20 and no negative results.

## Conceptual progression and dependencies

Begin with a known whole and a fully explained removal, then obscure _only_ the original whole's numeral while keeping removed and remaining quantities visible. Reconstruct the original by combining two known parts, express the addition and restore the number into the original subtraction. Contrast positions of the blank without requiring formal symbolic rearrangement. Transfer to a fresh example with smaller prompts.

## Misconceptions, examples and boundaries

Common errors: answer 3 by subtracting 7 − 4 rather than reconstructing the whole; treat 7 as the whole because it is after the equals sign; add 4 to the hidden number twice; or check only 7 + 4, never 11 − 4. Use □ − 4 = 7 → 11; □ − 3 = 5 → 8. Contrasting 11 − □ = 7 is **not** the same unknown; its missing value is 4. Avoid negative, fractional, multi-unknown and algebraic-transformation tasks.
