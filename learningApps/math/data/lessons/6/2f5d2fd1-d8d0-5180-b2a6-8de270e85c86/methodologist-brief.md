# Make Groups of a Given Size — Methodologist Brief

## Educational purpose

Introduce quotitive division as discovering the NUMBER of equal-sized groups that fit a total when the SIZE of each group is already given. Show the contrast with the prior fair-sharing story: 12 ÷ 3 = 4 now means 'how many groups of three can be made?' rather than 'how many in each of three groups?'. The arithmetic is the same but the units of the input and answer change.

## Target audience

Grade 2, selected for readiness to count and partition groups; the canonical Skill has no grade ceiling. Students need concrete models and short translated prompts, not timed fact recall.

## Prerequisites

- Count and make small equal groups, such as repeated collections of three.
- Understand that a fixed total can be partitioned without adding or losing objects.
- Recognize the distinction between 'groups of size three' and 'three groups' from the equal-sharing model.

## Expected learning outcomes

- Build four groups of size three from twelve objects and identify four as the number of complete groups.
- Read 12 ÷ 3 = 4 with 'three in each group' as the known divisor and 'four groups' as the quotient in this story.
- Explain that grouping 14 by fours yields three complete groups with two left, not a fourth incomplete group counted as complete.
- Build groups of size five from twenty counters, count four complete groups, and check 4×5=20.

## Essential subject matter

Quotitive division knows total 12 and group size 3, and determines four groups, written 12÷3=4 because 3+3+3+3=12. This story differs from 12 shared AMONG 3 groups, where 12÷3=4 reports four in each. Both are valid interpretations with different units for divisor and quotient. If total 14 and fixed size 4, create 3 complete groups (12 counters) with 2 left over; no statement 14÷4=3 as an exact whole-number equality. Transfer 20 objects at 5 each gives 4 complete groups.

## Conceptual progression and dependencies

1. **Build Groups of Three:** Start 12 independent counter IDs and a 'Make next group of 3' native control. Child creates four labeled groups of exactly three while pool counts 12→9→6→3→0, each group using exactly one set of three.
2. **Same Symbols, Different Question:** Present two explicitly labeled models with the exact same 12÷3=4 equality: partitive sharing makes 3 groups of 4; grouping makes 4 groups of 3. Learner chooses which number describes groups versus counters in each, without silently changing either story.
3. **When Some Do Not Fit:** New 14-counter pool with target group size four; 'Pack a complete group' produces three groups of 4, leaves 2 in source. A fourth group cannot be formed. Show explicitly '3 complete groups and 2 left'.
4. **Build Groups of Five:** Transfer with pool 20, group size 5, learner predicts 3/4/5, groups via accessible action and checks four groups use all twenty.

## Misconceptions, examples and boundaries

Confusing a requested group size with a required number of groups; counting an incomplete group as a full group; constructing groups of four instead of groups of three; thinking three groups of three uses all twelve; treating quotient units as always items-per-group. No formal remainder algorithm, negative numbers, fractions or grouping by zero.

## Localization, accessibility and offline intent

Every child-facing string must be genuinely localized, without silent English fallback. Distinguish the group COUNT from group SIZE in all languages; RTL must not reverse the mathematical meaning of expressions. Countable objects and their states have complete keyboard/screen-reader/table-ledger equivalents, full native touch controls, focus, zoom, reduced motion and offline deterministic calculations after downloading selected-language resources. No motor-precision drag or audio-only instructions.
