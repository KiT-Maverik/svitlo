## Educational purpose

Read exact hours on analog clocks using the hands correctly and match those readings to digital time. The intended conceptual change is from judging by appearance or one tempting example to checking the specific mathematical condition. This lesson serves one independently assessable canonical Skill without altering its identity.

## Target audience

**Primary grade: Grade 1**, approximately ages 6–7. Whole-hour readings suit early primary numeracy when digits 1–12 and clock hands are familiar; exact-minute reading can be deferred. Assume mixed literacy and language fluency: child-facing phrasing must be short and supported by visible and nonvisual equivalents.

## Prerequisites

- Identify the numerals 1 through 12.
- Distinguish a short hand from a long hand through length or accessible labels.
- Recognize that a circular dial has twelve labelled hour positions.

Prerequisite competence is a planning assumption, not evidence of mastery. A learner lacking it needs a separate scaffold, not a new definition of the target Skill.

## Expected learning outcomes

- Identify the hour hand and minute hand independently.
- Read exact-hour analog displays such as 3:00 and 8:00.
- Match an exact-hour analog display to a digital form ending in :00.
- Explain why the long hand pointing to 12 is not a reading of twelve minutes.

Completing an exploratory interaction is not independent validation of mastery. Any later formal evidence or scoring belongs to an assessment design.

## Essential subject matter and instructional depth

Read exact 1:00–12:00 analog times and matching digital :00 times. Note that the hour hand moves continuously in real clocks but do not teach intermediate readings. Exclude 24-hour notation, morning/evening inference, elapsed intervals, minute counting and time zones.

Canonical readHour Skill directly relates hour and minute hand positions to matching digital notation and cautions that the hour hand moves continuously.

## Conceptual progression and dependencies

Start from the listed prerequisites, identify what information determines the answer, make a prediction, reveal a controlling comparison, and explain it before transfer. Display analog and digital clocks together, use a manually controlled clock with labeled hands, and use nonvisual hand-position descriptions rather than color or rotation alone. Conceptual depth is set for this lesson's grade only; the Skill taxonomy remains grade-independent.

## Misconceptions, examples and boundaries

- The long hand indicates the hour.
- A long hand at 12 means 12 minutes after the hour.
- The short hand jumps instantaneously from one number to the next.
- A clock reading alone always identifies morning or evening.

Concrete boundaries: Read exact 1:00–12:00 analog times and matching digital :00 times. Note that the hour hand moves continuously in real clocks but do not teach intermediate readings. Exclude 24-hour notation, morning/evening inference, elapsed intervals, minute counting and time zones. Repeated exposure alone must not be presented as mastery.
