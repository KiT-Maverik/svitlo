## Educational purpose

Formulate and identify statistical questions whose answers depend on varied observations or responses, rather than a single fixed arithmetic calculation or known fact.

**Why this lesson now:** The earlier collection lessons supplied counting and recording mechanics. This Lesson makes the question driving collection explicit before learners interpret more complex charts. The canonical Skill remains an independently assessable unit even though this Lesson structures examples and models around it.

## Target audience

**Primary grade: Grade 2**, approximately ages 7–8. Grade 2: pupils have already counted categories and used frequency tables; now they can explicitly frame an investigation. The grade and learning depth are editorial decisions and do not redefine the Skill's cross-grade identity. Use short child-native language and meaningful equivalent interactions for visual, nonvisual, mobile and multilingual learners.

## Prerequisites

- Recognize a question as a request for information.
- Count and sort simple categorical responses from a small fictional group.
- Understand that different people can give different answers to the same preference or observation question.

These are assumed competencies, not claims that a preceding Playground Lesson proves mastery. Missing prerequisites require separate support.

## Expected learning outcomes

- Recognize that ‘Which fruit do the children in this fictional group choose?’ requires several responses that could vary.
- Distinguish a data investigation from ‘What is 4 + 3?’ with one predetermined arithmetic answer.
- Ask a clear data question naming the group and what each person or object contributes.
- Choose a relevant response category for a short fictional preference survey without needing private personal information.
- Explain that an investigation question is valid even if collected answers happen to be the same.

Observe explanations and transfer behaviors, not mere clicks or answer reveals. Formal assessment and scores are outside this editorial batch.

## Essential subject matter and depth

A statistical question anticipates variability and needs a specified set of observations/responses. Two-way classification by fruit preference is a child-friendly case. Mathematical problems with a known unique answer, such as 4 + 3, are not statistical investigations. An observational count of one fixed image can be data collection but is not necessarily a question anticipating variability among members of a group. Do not claim the difference is simply ‘a question with numbers’ versus ‘a question without numbers.’

**Verified model cases:** Use the fictional group A–F with six responses to ‘Which fruit would you pick today?’: apple, banana, apple, pear, banana, apple. The categories are three apples, two bananas, one pear. A separate fictional group where all six choose apples is still a valid example of a potentially variable survey question.

## Conceptual progression and dependencies

Recall the relevant earlier model, ask a precise prediction, allow an active inspection, reveal a mathematically justified counterexample, then offer a new transfer condition. A four-step inquiry builder with fixed invented questions, a choice of fictional respondent group and attribute, and transparent response lists; no network, account data, personal survey or dynamic user-content persistence. The Screenwriter can adjust pacing within this conceptual boundary after Methodologist approval.

## Misconceptions, examples and boundaries

- Any question mentioning a number is automatically a data investigation.
- A question can be investigated without stating which group or observations matter.
- All survey answers must differ for the question to count as statistical.
- Children must share real private preferences to practise a survey.
- A question with a known arithmetic answer requires a survey.

**Explicit exclusions:** No representative sampling claims, margins of error, generalizations to a population, inferential statistics, percentages, charts, real learner profiles or collection/storage of actual personal data.
