## Educational purpose

Classify even and odd counts by dividing objects into disjoint pairs, explaining when an item is left without a partner and why arrangement cannot change parity.

## Target audience

**Primary grade: Grade 2.** This selection depends on mathematical complexity, prerequisites and developmental readiness, not a grade label embedded in Skill Taxonomy. Early readers can use short instructions, countable models and ordered text alternatives.

## Prerequisites

Count distinct items to at least fifteen and intentionally pair two distinct objects with each pair member used once.

## Target Skills

- [evenOdd.data.ts](../../../../data/skills/counting/parity/skills/evenOdd.data.ts)

## Expected learning outcomes

- Eight is even, zero leftover.
- Nine is odd, one leftover.
- Twelve remains even.
- Thirteen is odd.
- Explain a changed-context instance using evidence rather than repeating a memorized answer; formative success is not proof of verified mastery.

## Essential subject matter

Eight objects form four disjoint pairs and no leftover: even. Nine form four pairs and one unmatched object: odd. Twelve form six pairs whether regular or scattered: even. Thirteen form six pairs and one unmatched object: odd. Zero is even mathematically, but introduced only as optional extension.

## Conceptual progression and dependencies

Introduce prerequisite number and object meanings → construct and inspect the first model → explain its contrast → retrieve the mathematical principle → independently apply it in the second Scene with a changed arrangement or representation.

## Misconceptions, examples and boundaries

Confusing four pairs with four objects; letting the same object belong to two pairs; thinking that an untidy layout must be odd; concluding odd while two unpaired objects could still form another pair.

**Instructional depth and hard exclusions:** Grade 2 concrete collections of 1–15 items; no negative integers, formal divisibility test, memorized final-digit shortcut or scoring.

**Verified numerical examples:**

- 8=4 pairs +0 leftover.
- 9=4 pairs +1 leftover.
- 12=6 pairs +0 both ways.
- 13=6 pairs +1 leftover.
