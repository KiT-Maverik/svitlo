## Educational purpose

Teach the independent fact that an amount subtracted from itself leaves zero, a − a = 0, grounded in complete removal, paired matching or inverse addition. It differs from subtracting zero, which leaves the initial amount.

## Target audience

Children aged 6–8, grades 1–2, who count whole-number sets within 20 and know that subtraction describes removal or difference. Keep explanations short and models accessible without vision or fine motor dragging.

## Prerequisites

- Recognize zero as none, count small nonnegative collections, and relate whole and parts.
- Identify what amount is removed and what remains; read simple subtraction equalities.

## Expected learning outcomes

- Demonstrate and explain that 6 − 6 = 0 by removing the entire starting group.
- Generalize to a − a = 0 for additional nonnegative examples including 12 − 12 and 0 − 0.
- Distinguish the roles of numbers in a−a=0 from a−0=a and from a case of partial removal.
- Check an example with inverse addition: 0 + 6 = 6.

## Essential subject matter

The first and second operands are equal, so the entire starting amount is removed; no counters remain. In the nonnegative whole-number model, for each a, a−a=0. Boundary a=0 yields 0−0=0. The general algebraic explanation involving additive inverses is unnecessary at ages six to eight; remain faithful to the canonical mathematical fact. This is not the claim that every subtraction yields zero, nor that subtracting zero removes everything.

## Conceptual progression and dependencies

Start with six and remove six countable objects; compare with removing only two or removing none; then extend to twelve without requiring counting one by one. Use the inverse addition check to link result zero with the original whole. End with a child-created example.

## Misconceptions, examples and boundaries

Confusing 6−6 with 6−0; assuming “numbers look alike” means zero in addition too; believing a zero answer requires starting with zero; double-counting removed objects as remaining; treating subtraction as commutative. Use positive quantities up to 20 and a=0 as a natural boundary. Exclude negative numbers and formal algebra transformations.

## Localization, accessibility and offline intent

Provide count ledger before/removed/after and accessible buttons for removing all or none; no color, visual disappearance, audio, timed or drag-only mode. Translate “all,” “none,” “from itself” accurately; RTL-safe arithmetic and fully local offline model.
