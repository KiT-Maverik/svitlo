## Educational purpose

Recognize actual denominations in an explicitly named euro currency case study, and distinguish face value from physical size or national-side design.

## Target audience

**Primary grade: Grade 2.** This selection depends on mathematical complexity, prerequisites and developmental readiness, not a grade label embedded in Skill Taxonomy. Early readers can use short instructions, countable models and ordered text alternatives.

## Prerequisites

Recognize numerals up to twenty, basic comparisons, and a supplied bridge that one euro equals 100 cents.

## Target Skills

- [recognizeDenominations.data.ts](../../../../data/skills/money/denominations/skills/recognizeDenominations.data.ts)

## Expected learning outcomes

- €2 is a coin and €5 a note.
- Both are worth €2.
- €1 is worth more though 50c coin is physically wider.
- Real €5 note; no standard ordinary €5 euro coin.
- Explain a changed-context instance using evidence rather than repeating a memorized answer; formative success is not proof of verified mastery.

## Essential subject matter

Euro coins: 1,2,5,10,20,50 cents, and one/two euros. Europa-series banknotes include five and ten euros. Standard circulating five-euro coin does not exist. Fifty-cent coin diameter 24.25 mm exceeds one-euro coin diameter 23.25 mm, but one euro equals 100 cents and exceeds 50 cents in value.

## Conceptual progression and dependencies

Introduce prerequisite number and object meanings → construct and inspect the first model → explain its contrast → retrieve the mathematical principle → independently apply it in the second Scene with a changed arrangement or representation.

## Misconceptions, examples and boundaries

Reading 50 as greater than 1 without converting units; guessing worth from coin size; treating a five-euro banknote as proof a five-euro coin must exist; assuming national-side artwork changes worth.

**Instructional depth and hard exclusions:** Grade 2 real euro case study; core items one- and two-euro coins plus five- and ten-euro notes; 50-cent contrast supported by explicit conversion reference. No changing currency automatically with UI locale, no purchase or exchange rates, no unlicensed banknote art.

**Verified numerical examples:**

- €1/€2 coins; €5/€10 banknotes.
- €2 remains €2 irrespective of national-side artwork.
- 50c width 24.25mm>€1 width 23.25mm; monetary 50c<100c.
- €5 banknote exists; standard €5 coin does not.
