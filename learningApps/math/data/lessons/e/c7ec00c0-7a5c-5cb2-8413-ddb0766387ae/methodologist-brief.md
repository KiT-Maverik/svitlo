## Prerequisite knowledge and Skills

Notice straight and curved edges in drawings; distinguish same/different visually or with tactile equivalents. Counting to four helpful but supported.

## Expected observable learning outcomes

- Identify familiar flat figures despite turning, resizing or recoloring.
- Justify identification using curved boundary, straight sides and corners where appropriate.
- Explain that every square is a rectangle, although not every rectangle is a square.

## Essential mathematical ideas

Identity depends on geometric properties, not visual decoration. A square has four equal straight sides and four right angles, so it satisfies the rectangle's defining right-angle condition. Early examples do not require formal proof.

## Conceptual progression

Start with a plausible misconception, invite a prediction, make the relevant invariant or comparison observable, provide controlled learner manipulation, explain counterexamples, and finish with a fresh independent application. Short formative responses are not proof of mastery.

## Common misconceptions and boundaries

A rotated square can look like a 'diamond'; children may reject a square as a rectangle; color or size can dominate attention.

Exclude unneeded symbolic formalism and unrelated geometry or measurement topics. Do not change any Skill definition to match these illustrations.
