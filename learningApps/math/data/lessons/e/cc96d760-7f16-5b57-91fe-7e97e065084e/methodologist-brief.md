## Educational purpose

Build an independently assessable understanding that taking away no amount leaves the starting quantity unchanged: a − 0 = a. Distinguish the order-sensitive meaning of zero as subtrahend; do not teach subtraction as commutative.

## Target audience

Children 6–8, grades 1–2, who count small collections and already know that subtraction removes an amount. Short texts, visual and nonvisual object models, optional child-led manipulation.

## Prerequisites

- Recognize 0 as “none,” count nonnegative quantities to 20, read basic subtraction.
- Understand initial amount, amount taken away, and what remains.

## Expected learning outcomes

- Predict and explain why 7 − 0 = 7 and 13 − 0 = 13 using an unchanged collection or number line.
- Interpret zero as the amount removed and identify the unchanged starting number as the difference.
- Use a − 0 = a for examples within 20 while explaining the invariance, not only reciting a rule.
- Recognize that reversing a subtraction generally changes its meaning, and that 0 − 7 is not equal to 7 or an ordinary take-away action on zero objects in this nonnegative model.

## Essential subject matter

Subtraction by zero leaves the minuend unchanged. A display of seven objects with “remove none” retains seven objects; a number-line jump of length zero stays at seven. Removing zero from zero leaves zero. In contrast, the expression 0 − 7 asks to subtract seven from zero; its integer result is −7, outside the lesson’s nonnegative-number domain. It must not be presented as equal to seven or as a valid removal of seven counters from an empty collection. This Skill does not teach the separate general rule a−a=0.

## Conceptual progression and dependencies

Predict before a zero-removal animation; confirm the count; compare with removing a positive number; transfer to another starting number and check with addition: 7−0=7 and 7+0=7.

## Misconceptions, examples and boundaries

A child may equate subtracting zero with reaching zero, reverse operands, treat zero as a magic eraser, or think a no-motion number-line jump changes quantity. Contrasts: 7−0=7 versus 7−2=5, not 0−7=7. Keep examples nonnegative with initial quantities ≤20; negative results are described only as outside the current model.

## Localization, accessibility and offline intent

“None removed” must remain distinct from “zero remaining”; zero jumps and stationary counters must have full spoken/text alternatives, not rely on animation. Keyboard, RTL-safe notation, readable focus, countable text and offline deterministic feedback mandatory.
