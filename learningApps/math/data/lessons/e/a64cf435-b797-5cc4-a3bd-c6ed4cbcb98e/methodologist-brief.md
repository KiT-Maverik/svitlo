# What Do We Know? What Must We Find? — Methodologist Brief

## Educational purpose

Teach learners to separate given quantities, their relationships and the precise question from a word problem before deciding on a calculation.

## Target audience

**Primary audience: Grade 1.** Use short clear instructions, optional audio read-aloud, large touch targets and a nonvisual text representation. This is an instructional depth decision for the Lesson, not a grade restriction on the canonical Skill.

## Prerequisites

- Count within twenty; understand 'altogether', 'left' and 'more' in familiar situations; comprehend short spoken or readable story sentences.

## Target Skills

- [identifyKnownUnknown.data.ts](../../../../data/skills/problemSolving/conditions/skills/identifyKnownUnknown.data.ts)

## Expected learning outcomes

- Name the quantity asked for, identify provided quantitative facts and their relationships, state the unit where present, and explain why a decorative detail is not automatically a numeric fact.

## Essential subject matter

In 'A box has 6 pencils. Sam puts in 3 more. How many now?', known values are 6 pencils, 3 additional pencils and the increase relationship; unknown is the new total number of pencils. A name, color or scene setting is not numerical evidence. The word 'more' alone cannot tell which arithmetic operation to apply in every problem.

## Conceptual progression and dependencies

First unpack a pictorial spoken problem; next label given/asked in a short textual example; then independently interpret a structurally different story without carrying out a mandatory calculation.

## Misconceptions, examples and boundaries

Do not mistake a number mentioned in the question for an already known result. Do not assume every word or numeral must be used. Do not teach a keyword-to-operation shortcut; avoid tasks requiring multiplication or two-step plans here.

## Localization, accessibility and offline intent

The English examples are editorial source text; genuine translations must be authored for every supported locale before implementation. All quantities, relationships, labels, hints and feedback must remain available in text and not only color, movement or audio. Preserve mathematical relationship order in RTL; do not blindly mirror a number line. Every action needs keyboard and touch alternatives, visible focus, 200% zoom and screen-reader descriptions. Illustrations must have equivalent descriptions; motion is optional. Deliver complete instructional media for offline use; do not promise Note persistence or scoring.
