## Educational purpose

Count forward from a chosen start within 20, including ten transitions, and read/write numerals connected to quantities 0–20. Program-neutral and independently teachable.

## Target audience

**Grade 1 (primary audience)**, early readers using short prompts, countable illustrations, and optional narration. No requirement for mouse use, hearing, or fluent reading.

## Prerequisites

Identify digits 0–9, one-to-one count through ten and understand cardinality. Provide an optional introductory reminder, not an assumption of demonstrated mastery.

## Expected learning outcomes

1. Nine then one more makes ten, written 10.
2. 10+3 = 13; 10+7 = 17; 10+0 = 10.
3. Each forward count adds one; 19 is followed by 20.
4. 14 labels fourteen crayons; 10+4 is fourteen.

- Explain a new instance using the underlying number relationship, not merely identify a familiar arrangement.

## Essential subject matter and instructional depth

Concrete full ten-frame and extra units; no generalized place-value algorithms or carrying. Verified model examples:

- 9, 10.
- Ten and three → 13.
- 17, 18, 19, 20.
- Ten plus four → 14.

## Conceptual progression and dependencies

1. After nine: Fill tenth cell of a ten-frame and reveal the numeral 10.
2. Ten and some more: Explore a full ten-frame plus 3, 7 or zero loose beads; select numeral and count aloud or via ordered ledger.
3. Beyond nineteen: Continue 17,18,19,20 on a 0–20 number strip, one step per activation.
4. Crayon label: Match an unfamiliar full-ten-plus-four crayon set to 14, contrasting 12 and 21.

## Misconceptions, examples and boundaries

After 19 comes 110, thirteen is written 103, reversed two-digit numerals are equivalent, all counting must restart at one. Feedback must point to the relevant concrete counting evidence. No score, timed drill, or inference of mastered competence.
