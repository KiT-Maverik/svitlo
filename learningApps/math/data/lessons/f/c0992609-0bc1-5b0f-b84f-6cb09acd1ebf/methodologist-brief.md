## Educational purpose

Same length, different number: the unit matters. This Lesson is an autonomous introduction to its assigned canonical competency; it must be valuable without a Program storyline or assessments.

## Target audience and depth

**Primary grade: 2** (approximate ages 7–8). Early or mixed readers, multilingual learners, keyboard/switch users, low-vision users and learners who need concrete examples must receive equivalent access. **Instructional depth:** Whole nonstandard units only, no gaps/overlaps; compare unit-size effect, distinguish numerical unit count from physical length. ## Expected observable learning outcomes

- Lay identical unit tiles end-to-end to cover a straight length without gaps or overlaps.
- Count complete unit intervals accurately and identify misleading measurements.
- Explain why a shorter unit produces a larger unit count for the same fixed object.

## Essential mathematical ideas

Measurement iterates one fixed unit to partition a length. A bar of length 12 grid spaces is 6 two-space tiles or 4 three-space tiles long. The physical bar is unchanged despite different numeric counts.

## Conceptual progression

Start with a plausible misconception, invite a prediction, make the relevant invariant or comparison observable, provide controlled learner manipulation, explain counterexamples, and finish with a fresh independent application. Short formative responses are not proof of mastery.

## Common misconceptions and boundaries

Counting boundary ticks rather than intervals; leaving gaps, stacking tiles or changing unit size midway; assuming more units means physically longer.

Exclude unneeded symbolic formalism and unrelated geometry or measurement topics. Do not change any Skill definition to match these illustrations.
