## Educational purpose

Use the final count word to describe the total quantity of a collection, independent of arrangement. Program-neutral and independently teachable.

## Target audience

**Grade 1 (primary audience)**, early readers using short prompts, countable illustrations, and optional narration. No requirement for mouse use, hearing, or fluent reading.

## Prerequisites

Reliable one-to-one counting to ten and a stable number-word sequence. Provide an optional introductory reminder, not an assumption of demonstrated mastery.

## Target Skills

- [basicCardinality.data.ts](../../../../data/skills/counting/cardinality/skills/basicCardinality.data.ts)

## Expected learning outcomes

1. The last count word, four, describes all four shells.
2. The set remains four because no shell was added or removed.
3. Zero marbles in the empty bowl, one after adding, zero after removing.
4. Eight is the quantity of the whole collection.

- Explain a new instance using the underlying number relationship, not merely identify a familiar arrangement.

## Essential subject matter and instructional depth

Empty and nonempty sets 0–12, concrete models; no symbolic arithmetic drills. Verified model examples:

- One, two, three, four → four shells in all.
- Four together = four spread apart.
- Zero marbles means none.
- Eight pencils altogether.

## Conceptual progression and dependencies

1. Four shells: Count four shells one by one, then answer without recounting.
2. Spread them apart: Predict, spread four shells, and optionally recount the same identified objects.
3. Empty bowl: Inspect an empty bowl, add a marble, then remove it.
4. A pencil box: Independently count eight pencils in a different layout, give total with number or word choices.

## Misconceptions, examples and boundaries

Final count word names only the last object, wider spacing means more objects, counting the container instead of its contents. Feedback must point to the relevant concrete counting evidence. No score, timed drill, or inference of mastered competence.
