## Educational purpose

Use the same conserved monetary value to compose different valid combinations and to compare purses by summed denominations, not count or size of cash pieces.

## Target audience

**Primary grade: Grade 2.** This selection depends on mathematical complexity, prerequisites and developmental readiness, not a grade label embedded in Skill Taxonomy. Early readers can use short instructions, countable models and ordered text alternatives.

## Prerequisites

Recognize real euro €1/€2 coins and €5/€10 banknotes; add visible whole-number values within twenty and understand that piece count differs from money worth.

## Expected learning outcomes

- €5+€1=€6 with two pieces.
- Three coins still sum €6, equal in value to two-piece combination.
- €3 is a valid total, not an ordinary euro coin denomination.
- B is higher: €11>€10.
- A is €7 versus B €6.
- B has more worth: €5 versus €4.
- Explain a changed-context instance using evidence rather than repeating a memorized answer; formative success is not proof of verified mastery.

## Essential subject matter

€6=€5+€1 (two pieces)=€2+€2+€2 (three pieces)=€2+€2+€1+€1 (four pieces). €3 amount can be €2+€1 but no standard €3 coin exists. Purse A €10 note vs B €5 note+three €2 coins =€11; purse A €5+€2=€7 vs B three €2 coins =€6; two €2 coins =€4 versus a €5 banknote =€5.

## Conceptual progression and dependencies

Introduce prerequisite number and object meanings → construct and inspect the first model → explain its contrast → retrieve the mathematical principle → independently apply it in the second Scene with a changed arrangement or representation.

## Misconceptions, examples and boundaries

Counting coins as euros regardless of value; five-euro note as five notes; assuming more pieces means higher value; using a fictional €3 euro coin; assuming different combinations must have different totals.

**Instructional depth and hard exclusions:** Grade 2, 0–15 whole euros; legitimate €1 and €2 coins, €5 and €10 notes; no cents, rates, transactions, purchase totals, budgets, change, scoring, or automatically substituted locale-specific currency.

**Verified numerical examples:**

- €5+€1=€6.
- €2+€2+€2=€6.
- €2+€1=€3; no standard €3 coin.
- A €10, B €11 → B greater.
- A €7, B €6 → A greater.
- A €4, B €5 → B greater.
