## Educational purpose

Develop two distinct multiplication laws with evidence from small groups and numerical reasoning: multiplying a whole number by zero gives zero, while multiplying it by one leaves the same number. Neither property means that adding or subtracting zero works in the same way.

## Target audience

Children aged 6–8 who can interpret small multiplication expressions and distinguish zero objects, one group and one in each group. This is an autonomous Lesson and does not presume particular Program or class placement.

## Prerequisites

- Recognize 0 as no objects and 1 as one object or one group, with clear role descriptions.
- Read equal-group multiplication as group count × group size and check a small total.
- Count small sets, including an empty set, and read a simple numerical equality.

## Expected learning outcomes

- Explain 4×0=0 with four groups that each contain zero items, and 0×4=0 with zero groups of four.
- Explain 4×1=4 with four groups of one, and 1×4=4 with one group of four.
- Apply a×0=0 and a×1=a to further whole-number examples without needing a picture every time.
- Distinguish 4+0=4 from 4×0=0, identifying the operation as well as the special factor.

## Essential subject matter

Zero is absorbing for multiplication: a × 0 = 0 (also 0 × a = 0); one is the multiplicative identity: a × 1 = a (and 1 × a = a). Use 4×0=0, 0×4=0, 4×1=4, 1×4=4 and transfer 7×0=0, 7×1=7. Concrete equal-group models demonstrate cases but are not claimed to prove the law for every number domain; the source Skill's formal generality remains unchanged. Keep age-appropriate whole numbers 0–20.

## Conceptual progression and dependencies

Contrasting empty and nonempty groups → see four groups with zero in each → compare four groups of one against one group of four → reason about new whole numbers and contrast multiplication with addition.

## Misconceptions, examples and boundaries

Claiming four groups of zero have four objects; confusing zero groups with one empty group; thinking any operation with zero produces zero, so 4+0=0; thinking ×1 adds one to the original; treating zero as an invalid multiplication factor because division by zero is forbidden. Do not conflate multiplication by zero with division by zero.
