## Educational purpose

An object can stay still while its position words change when the observer walks around it. This Lesson is an autonomous introduction to its assigned canonical competency; it must be valuable without a Program storyline or assessments.

## Target audience and depth

**Primary grade: 1** (approximate ages 6–7). Early or mixed readers, multilingual learners, keyboard/switch users, low-vision users and learners who need concrete examples must receive equivalent access. **Instructional depth:** Near/far, above/below, in front/behind, left/right from a named viewer; one reference at a time. No coordinate grids or complex routes.

## Prerequisite knowledge and Skills

Understand that an object can be named and located in a scene; distinguish an observer from the objects being described. No fluent reading assumed.

- No formal prerequisite Skill ID is claimed; foundational experiences are described above.

## Expected observable learning outcomes

- Describe a location using an explicit reference object and appropriate relational words.
- Explain why left/right or front/behind may change when the viewer changes position.
- Follow and correct a short position instruction without relying on the object's color.

## Essential mathematical ideas

Spatial expressions describe relationships, not intrinsic properties. 'The cup is to the left of the book' must specify a viewpoint when relevant. For unambiguous above/below on a flat scene, distinguish screen position from real 3D front/behind.

## Conceptual progression

Start with a plausible misconception, invite a prediction, make the relevant invariant or comparison observable, provide controlled learner manipulation, explain counterexamples, and finish with a fresh independent application. Short formative responses are not proof of mastery.

## Common misconceptions and boundaries

Children may describe 'left' without naming whose left; mistake perspective for a change in object location; confuse above with in front of.

Exclude unneeded symbolic formalism and unrelated geometry or measurement topics. Do not change any Skill definition to match these illustrations.
