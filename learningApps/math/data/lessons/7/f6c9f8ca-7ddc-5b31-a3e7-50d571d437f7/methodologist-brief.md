## Educational purpose

Develop the capacity to represent a one-step quantitative situation with meaningful groups, bars or expressions, keeping the unknown distinct from known information.

## Target audience

**Primary audience: Grade 2.** Use short clear instructions, optional audio read-aloud, large touch targets and a nonvisual text representation. This is an instructional depth decision for the Lesson, not a grade restriction on the canonical Skill.

## Prerequisites

- Identify known/unknown quantities in a word problem, add/subtract within twenty and distinguish a part from a whole.

## Target Skills

- [oneStepModel.data.ts](../../../../data/skills/problemSolving/models/skills/oneStepModel.data.ts)

## Expected learning outcomes

- Construct a concrete or diagrammatic representation preserving quantities and relationships; write a matching single-operation expression; distinguish a model that represents the story from a visually similar but incorrect one; interpret the result with its unit.

## Essential subject matter

For '7 apples are on a plate and 5 are added', model the initial 7 and the additional 5 as parts of an unknown total: 7 + 5 = ?. For '12 apples, 5 are eaten', total 12 is known, removal 5 is known and remaining number is unknown: 12 − 5 = ?. A correct model must preserve the roles of whole, parts and direction of change, rather than merely display both numerals.

## Conceptual progression and dependencies

Connect narrative to movable counters, then label a part-whole or before/change/after diagram, then select the matching one-step mathematical expression without depending on superficial keywords.

## Misconceptions, examples and boundaries

Two bars of different lengths are not inherently meaningful without an explicit scale. Do not encode 'more' as always add. Avoid two-step, multiplicative, fractional or proportional problems. Graphical lengths can mislead when proportions are inconsistent; provide labeled equal-unit counters.
