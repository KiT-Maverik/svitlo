# Lesson

**Entity category:** self-contained, Course-neutral educational material. Can be standalone, reused across Courses, or authored under one Course without acquiring narrative responsibilities.

A **Lesson** independently teaches **one or more approved Skills**. It must make sense without a Program, Curriculum, mascot, quest, reward system, external plot or special learner history. The Lesson owns the actual instructional material and its purposeful order of learning experiences; a Program can place it into a larger journey but does not own its pedagogical content.

## PEDAGOGICAL RESPONSIBILITIES

- Present accurate content through appropriate explanations, demonstrations, interactions, guided practice and reflection. No fixed Section count, fixed `theory → practice → quiz` recipe or one-Skill-per-Lesson rule.
- Link each Lesson's intended learning to the Methodologist's approved outcomes and Skill definitions; teaching, completing a Lesson, marking a checkpoint and demonstrating mastery are **different** concepts.
- A Lesson can have an **instructional sequence** and staged revelation. It has **no Program narrative** (characters, missions, external story, points/rewards or quest progression). See [Program](./program.md).
- Lesson content is assembled from ordered [Sections](./section.md) with typed resources. Its educational design is documented in the separate [Lesson Content](./lesson-content.md), authored by the Educational Screenwriter.

## Methodology quality contract — implementation-ready Lesson specification

**Lesson `methodology.md` is the complete, approved pedagogical technical specification**, not a short lesson summary or a request for the Educational Screenwriter to decide what should be taught. It defines the educational meaning and boundaries of the Lesson sufficiently precisely that a different specialist can author `content.yaml`, purposeful learning experiences, and assessment designs **without guessing, inventing or quietly broadening methodological requirements**. Methodology covers **what, why, for whom, prerequisites, depth and evidence**; Screenwriting and Engineering still decide **how** to implement those requirements.

For each Lesson, the Methodologist must provide substantive, concrete, verified answers to these obligations:

1. **Purpose and learner profile:** the educational problem addressed, intended learner/prior-knowledge profile, assumed language and literacy, developmental or professional context, relevant accessibility constraints, and why this Lesson should exist as a distinct reusable unit.
2. **Approved target Skills and measurable outcomes:** verified canonical Skill identities and source references; for **each** target Skill, an observable capability learners must develop, intended cognitive depth, scope of mastery and distinctions between initial exposure, supported practice and independently assessable evidence. A bare Skill ID or topic title is not an explanation of the Skill.
3. **Entry prerequisites and concept dependencies:** what learners must already know or be able to do, which ideas logically precede others, prerequisite terminology and representations, and how the Lesson should progress conceptually. Remain Course-neutral; do not depend on a particular Course chapter, storyline or mascot.
4. **Essential subject matter:** the actual key concepts, accurate definitions, relationships, rules, mechanisms, cases and substantive factual boundaries to be taught, **not** vague instructions such as "explain the topic". Include representative, age-appropriate worked examples and nonexamples, edge cases or counterexamples where these are needed for unambiguous teaching. Explain what must not be oversimplified or asserted.
5. **Pedagogical intent and misconceptions:** likely learner misconceptions and why they arise, how the target understanding differs from the wrong idea, meaningful demonstrations or models, practice/feedback needs and appropriate approaches to building or transferring understanding. Specify educational intent, **not** fixed UI widgets, Scene code, Section layouts or a mandatory presentation recipe.
6. **Explicit assessment contract:** mandatory frontmatter `assessmentPlan` deciding `quizRequired`; if true, individually justified `quizzes` with distinct keys, purposes, verified `targetSkills`, required `AssessmentMethodKey` operations and evidence intent as specified by the [Lesson Methodology frontmatter schema](../../skills/lesson-workflow/lesson-methodology-frontmatter.schema.json). If no Quiz is pedagogically needed, say `quizRequired: false` and `quizzes: []` and explain why. Define **what evidence should establish learning**, without inventing Question wording, correct answers, grading or retries.
7. **Adaptation and factual accountability:** relevant language/cultural adjustments, accessible equivalent ways to reach the same objective, safety/offline constraints, specific references and verified claims, known uncertainties, exclusions, and genuinely unresolved editorial decisions with an explicit blocker or owner.

**Completeness and handoff test:** Could an independent Screenwriter construct the full Lesson `content.yaml` and required production instructions from this Brief **without deciding any missing educational objective, factual scope, conceptual progression, misconception treatment, or assessment obligation**? If not, the Methodology is **not ready for approval**. A polished heading list, a few sentences per heading, or a generic "learn about X and test understanding" is insufficient. Conversely, an intrinsically small concept need not be padded to a quota: use as much detail as the specific subject needs, with no artificial token or word-count target. Cite sources and state remaining open decisions rather than making up certainty.

**Approval and revision ownership:** The Methodologist owns the substantive specification. The Screenwriter, Assessment Architect, translator, and Coding Agent must **implement** it within their roles, not opportunistically expand it. If production reveals missing or mistaken educational requirements, request a documented Methodologist/editor amendment and re-review of affected work; do not silently alter the Brief or treat a generated file as approval. The Lesson must remain useful on its own even when originally specified inside a Course plan.

**Proposed Course-plan handoff (not yet implemented):** When a new Course is planned in one Git-reviewed `course.yaml`, every planned Lesson's **complete Markdown methodological specification**, including verified Skills and `assessmentPlan`, would be authored and independently reviewed **before** Course materialization. The materializer would write the standalone Lesson `methodology.md` from that approved content without compressing or extrapolating it. The current [Create Lesson Brief](../../skills/lesson-workflow/create-lesson-brief.skill.md) remains the operative standalone authoring workflow until the proposed Course-plan process is implemented.

## EDITORIAL ARTIFACTS AND IMPLEMENTATION

Reusable/standalone Reusable/standalone Lesson files live under `<PackageRoot>/data/lessons/<last-hex-digit>/<uuid>/`. Course-owned (but still pedagogically Course-neutral) Lessons may be authored beneath their owning Course; see [Episode](./episode.md) for Course-owned narrative activities. The [shared UUID addressing rule](../architecture/entity-uuid-addressing.md) determines this path from the permanent Lesson ID. Keep the human-readable camelCase slug in Lesson metadata, not in the directory name. Existing folders do not move when a slug changes.

- `methodology.md` (legacy `methodologist-brief.md`): approved _what/why/for whom_, prerequisite, Skill scope and the Methodologist's explicit `assessmentPlan` (no Quiz, or one/more Quizzes with required `AssessmentMethodKey` values); an editorial-only source **not included in runtime configuration or client bundles**. Its `Target Skills` contains one direct relative Markdown link per Skill **to the canonical Skill `*.data.ts` path under `data/skills/`**, with the full source filename (including extension) as the link label. New Briefs **target `<PackageRoot>/data/skills/`** for Skill `*.data.ts` references. Validate every link to an existing source file. If that package has not migrated, report the gap as a blocker rather than writing broken links or pointing to `src/`.
- `content.yaml`: Screenwriter-owned **machine-readable** _how to teach_ plan with ordered Sections/ContentBlocks, learner-facing text in the declared `documentLanguage` and explicit assessment/Scene/media dependencies. Legacy `content.md` requires explicit migration; it is not a parallel canonical artifact.
- `scenes/`: separate Scene specifications **only when needed**, later consumed by the Scene implementation workflow.
- `questions.yaml`: Assessment Architect's approved source-language Question bank (specialist workflow, subject to separate editorial approval). It is the mandatory upstream input for Flashcard authoring; its future generator supplies canonical typed Question objects.
- `flashcards.yaml`: Flashcard Learning Architect's source-language recall bank, written **only after** approved Questions. Includes typed editorial ContentBlocks, resolvable Question field references, an explicit typed `flags` array (`list` count, `warning`, `acronym`, and `custom` value/color/optional icon), and a single canonical graph of card-to-card relations using the nine `FlashcardRelationType` keys. The Engine materializes approved YAML edges on both cards using the relationship model in [Flashcard schemas](../../../shared/engine/configurators/flashcards/defineFlashcard/defineFlashcard.schemas.ts), with stable partner IDs. The existing UI Kit Flashcard now renders typed flags automatically. Locale-specific acronym wording still requires independent translation; UI relationship navigation and scheduler semantics remain separate capabilities.
- Typed `*.data.ts` configuration and published resources are owned by implementation/build workflows, not by either editorial document.

**Observed contract on Draft PR #606:** [Engine Lesson Config and Props](../../../shared/engine/configurators/lessons/defineLesson/defineLesson.schemas.ts) contain stable Lesson identity, supported languages and ordered Sections, **not** embedded Questions or Flashcards. Quiz and Flashcards Sections refer to separately published resources by explicit UUID; an independent manifest records their owning Lesson and available languages. Folder addressing follows permanent UUIDs, not slugs. The full YAML production and cross-lesson migration are still pending; do not infer release readiness from these partial contracts.

Related: [Lesson Content](./lesson-content.md), [Section](./section.md), [Program](./program.md), [Skill](./skill.md).

## Typed authoring and publication boundary

Use [`defineLesson`](../../../shared/engine/configurators/lessons/defineLesson/defineLesson.ts) from `@svitlo/engine/build`. It composes the named, ordered `section` collection and declares `params.languages`; it does **not** own either bank. The `defineQuizSection` selection requires explicit `questionIds`; the `defineFlashcardsSection` selection requires explicit `flashcardIds`. Build-only independent Question and Flashcard configurators produce separately addressable Props, and the host supplies a selected-resource reader to UI Kit. Complete-publication referential validation and independent YAML authoring stages are still pending.

`lesson.build.props({ languages })` produces `base` and sparse `locale` Props fragments. `lesson.build.overrides(patch, { languages })` prepares Course-specific patches without mutating the Lesson. Generators, not configurators, write files.

At runtime, [`assembleLessonProps`](../../../shared/engine/assemblers/assembleLessonProps/assembleLessonProps.ts) merges the published base and selected locale with optional Course overrides and validates the complete Props.

**Editorial localization:** `content.yaml.documentLanguage` identifies the only locale populated by the initial YAML-to-entity generator; `supportedLanguages` sets the intended Lesson language tuple. Remaining locales require separate translation, and interim TypeScript errors must stay visible until translations exist. Initial generation is not proof of publication readiness.

## YAML TypeScript draft authoring handoff

The Screenwriter-owned `content.yaml` is an editorial plan; the technical `lesson.yaml` draft carries the authored Engine `defineLesson` Config tree, plus a removable source-language `documentLanguage` provenance field. The Engine YAML draft scaffolder converts it to incomplete named `*.data.ts` source using `@svitlo/engine/build` factories without validating, translating, typechecking or publishing. See [Config-shaped YAML handoff](../architecture/yaml-config-scaffolding.md). The Lesson Builder reads only `lesson.yaml`, excluding assessment banks; Course `config.yaml` stays unchanged. The published Props pipeline remains separate.


## Quest in standalone Lessons (planned runtime)

The [Quest Section](./quest.md) is designed for independently useful, accessible interactive demonstration and guided practice (e.g. manipulating a quadratic equation) without requiring characters or Course plot. It is not a Lesson subtype or a Quiz replacement. Quest Build contracts exist, but Lesson Section union, YAML and player integration are deferred. Do not publish a Quest as if supported; retain the approved Methodologist assessment and Skill boundaries.

### Independently authored Flashcards and Questions

The completed `defineFlashcard` Configurator retains typed flags, relationships and three structural sides; the front, back and optional hint use `defineFlashcardSide({ items, order })`. A side is *not* independently identified and must not receive an invented UUID. The separate `flashcards/index.ts` TypeScript bank (when present) is compiled into individual UUID-addressed JSON objects, not a `Lesson.flashcards` collection. The Section's explicit `flashcardIds` determines order.

The separately authored editorial YAML sources are `questions.yaml` and `flashcards.yaml` with independent authoring and review stages. The current typed-bank publisher does not yet implement YAML-to-TypeScript conversion. Refer to [the approved separation specification](../architecture/lesson-assessment-bank-separation.md) for the remaining work.
